# Brief

## Question

When a system displays a whole family of useful behaviours whose apparent design, learning, or evolutionary cost is far smaller than the scope of what it can do, where did the missing cost go? Michael Levin calls such cases "free lunches" from a Platonic space of patterns. Is the lunch free, and if not, who paid for it?

## Claim

There are no universally free lunches. There are domain-specific structural subsidies. A system pays a real cost to build an interface to a pre-existing regularity, mathematical, statistical, physical, developmental, collective, or historical, and then receives an entire family of consequences at low marginal cost. The disproportion between what the system explicitly paid and what it can now do is not a violation of thermodynamics or of the No-Free-Lunch theorems. It is the signature of a matched interface to a structured, nonuniform world. The scientifically useful question is therefore an accounting question: what structure supplied the discount, what interface accessed it, in which cost currency, and where was the remaining cost paid and stored.

The paper defends this by supplying what the intuition currently lacks: a vector-valued definition of a discount (across search, samples, description length, computation, energy, control, communication, construction, repair), an accounting boundary that names who paid and when, a two-axis taxonomy (source of subsidy by mechanism of discount) that reduces to seven accounting transformations (entailment, equivalence, compression, delegation, amortization, externalization, protection), four worked case studies at theorem-level, statistical, physical, and biological evidence, an inference ladder that blocks the slide from "interesting pattern" to "nonphysical mind" in one step, and a perturbational research program whose central control is structure-scrambling at fixed component count and energy. The stance is metaphysically open and operationally strict: the discounts are empirical, their ultimate ontology is left underdetermined, so the framework is usable by a reader who rejects Levin's Platonism and by one who accepts it.

The sharpest single move is the rename. "Discounted" is preferable to "free" because it forces the ledger to be written. Every strong case must exhibit a matching negative regime, a domain in which the same interface becomes neutral or harmful, or it is not a theory of conditional leverage.
