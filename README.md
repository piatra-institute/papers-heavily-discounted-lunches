# Heavily Discounted Lunches

Structural Subsidies and the Accounting of Competence. A synthesis paper that reframes Michael Levin's "free lunches" from a Platonic space of patterns as heavily discounted lunches: cases where the world supplies a pre-existing regularity (mathematical, statistical, physical, developmental, collective, or historical), a system pays a real cost to build an interface to it, and a family of consequences then arrives at low marginal cost, in no violation of thermodynamics or the No-Free-Lunch theorems. The paper supplies a vector-valued discount measure across cost currencies, an accounting boundary that records who paid and when, a taxonomy reducing to seven operations (entailment, equivalence, compression, delegation, amortization, externalization, protection), four case studies (NAND switching, Benford's law, the body as coprocessor, morphogenesis), an inference ladder that blocks the slide from a measured discount to a claim about a new ontology, and a perturbational research program whose central control is structure-scrambling at fixed component count and energy. No simulation; the numbers in the prose are cited results and framework definitions.
